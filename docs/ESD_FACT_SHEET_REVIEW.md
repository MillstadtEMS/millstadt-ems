# Millstadt EMS referendum: fact-sheet and flyer review

Reviewed October 2, 2026. Local review draft only. No GitHub push or Vercel deployment.

## Main findings

1. **The photographed $424 is a defensible rounded 0.40% gross tax, not the planned net increase.** The county's 2025 record for 305 Traver Tine Circle, parcel 12-10.0-414-002, reports $105,899 net taxable value. Multiplying that by 0.004 gives $423.596, or $423.60 to cents and $424 to whole dollars. This is a close numerical match; the flyer does not disclose a worksheet proving its author's exact method.
2. **The planned 0.30% comparison gives a $222.39 annual increase, conditional on replacing the 0.09% levy and holding value constant.** Current ambulance portion: $95.31. Planned levy: $317.70. Monthly equivalent increase: $18.53. These are calculated scenarios, not a guaranteed future extension.
3. **The flyer appears to use the post-exemption value.** EAV is $118,899; total approved exemptions are $13,000; net taxable value is $105,899. The printed 'before any exemptions' wording is crossed out. Using EAV before exemptions at 0.40% would give $475.60, not $424. Do not claim the flyer ignored exemptions.
4. **Your fact sheet misplaces the budget shortfalls under the 'No' discussion.** The referenced pro forma already includes $1,031,072.11 of tax revenue at 0.30%, with a 98% collection factor. Its $136,404.39 gap before replacement savings and $398,975.82 gap including savings are projected gaps even with that revenue. They are not verified losses under the current 0.09% funding or a calculated 'No' outcome.
5. **The flyer has support for some of its legal points.** The county's 2025 Millstadt report lists 0.2500% as the ambulance fund's maximum. Separate tax authority for eligible retirement, Social Security/Medicare, and liability costs also exists. Neither establishes that a particular increase has been approved or that a combined 0.50% rate will be levied.

## Review of your two-page fact sheet

| Original claim or wording | Assessment and correction |

| Financial plan uses 0.30% and generates about $1,031,072 at 98% collection | Matches the pro forma supplied in the connected website repository. Call it projected revenue. Neither a pro forma nor the existing EMS organization binds the future district's levy. |
| Taxes based on 'EAV' in the chart | Arithmetic is correct for all 12 listed rows, but label the input **taxable assessed value after exemptions**. EAV before exemptions is a different number. |
| Existing levy automatically ceases when the ESD is formed and starts operating | Too compressed. Section 2 sets out a Fire District resolution to cease the Section 22 ambulance levy and ESD operation of ambulance service in the overlapping territory. Verify the resolution and actual levy/collection timing; do not imply an election-day repeal or removal of fire-protection taxes. |
| Net change of 0.21% of the value | Mathematically sound if the input is taxable value, the old levy is replaced, and rates are exactly 0.09%/0.30%. '0.21 percentage-point rate difference' is clearer. |
| District boundaries; 'Smithton is not included' | The supplied ballot does not name Smithton Fire Protection District. Use the listed fire-district territories and certified boundary description, rather than treating a mailing address or village name as the legal boundary. |

| $136,404 operating gap / $398,976 including replacement savings under 'No' | The amounts reconcile to the 0.30% pro forma: $1,394,750.83 − $1,258,346.44 = $136,404.39; plus $262,571.43 replacement savings = $398,975.82. Move them to a clearly labeled proposed-budget section and state that the model still has a gap at 0.30%. |
| Could be unable to operate 'as early as next year' | The subsequent financial review identifies a depleted money-market account and overdue bills, but not all current bank balances, a complete current cash forecast, a board-approved shutdown plan or a closure date. Omit a specific date until substantiated; describe closure only as a conditional agency-reported risk. |
| 'No' does not automatically close service | A useful distinction. Also avoid promising that a particular provider, service level, staffing pattern, or contract will continue indefinitely. |
| Source cites sections 2 and 11.5 for the organizational rate | Use **70 ILCS 2005/2, /4, /5 and /11** for this discussion. Section 11.5 concerns separate additional referendum authority; it should not be the sole rate citation for the organizational question. |

## Review of the circulated flyer

| Claim | What the evidence supports |
| --- | --- |
| The ESD would be a new taxing body | Supported by the proposal and statute. |
| The ballot authorizes up to 0.40% | Supported. This is a real authorization that could be used; it is not merely an irrelevant hypothetical. The financial plan uses 0.30%, which is below the authorization. |
| $424 annual cost for this home | Consistent with $105,899 × 0.004, rounded. Distinguish a full new levy from the net increase. Under the same replacement assumption the increase at 0.40% is $328.29; at 0.30% it is $222.39. |
| Current ambulance rate 0.09%; up to 0.25% through the Fire District | The actual and maximum columns in the county's 2025 FDMI/064 report support those figures. A request is not approval, and available rate authority does not itself settle funding decisions or applicable extension procedures. |
| Voting No will not take away EMS; service will continue through the Fire District | Defeat does not itself dissolve existing ambulance service. The flyer does not establish an enforceable commitment to a particular provider, unchanged staffing, coverage, or future funding. Section 22 authorizes provision/contracting and states a public-service responsibility; it does not prove an indefinite contract with Millstadt EMS. |
| Seek competitive bids first | A policy proposal, not a financial result. No bid amounts, awarded contract, service specifications, or comparable staffing/response standards were supplied. Do not claim bids would necessarily save money, or necessarily cost more. |
| IMRF, Social Security, workers' comp and liability could bring total to 0.50% | There is legal authority for certain separate levies, subject to eligibility, authorized uses and other applicable rules. The reviewed statutes do not fix an automatic 0.10% surcharge. No adopted 0.50% levy or fund-by-fund extension was supplied. Conversely, do not claim that all additional levies are illegal or that 0.40% caps everything. |
| These employer costs must be newly added to the proposed budget | The flyer does not explicitly say that, so do not attribute this claim to its author. The EMS pro forma already lists IMRF, payroll taxes, workers' compensation and insurance. Budget inclusion alone does not prove whether the future board will fund them within the base levy or through separate authority. |

## Claims still requiring records

No supporting minutes, resolutions, correspondence, or offers were located in the materials reviewed. An asynchronous request for those document names/dates remains open. The public draft describes EMS's funding concern but does not assert that the Fire District denied requests as an independently verified fact.
- The signed cessation resolution, certified court order/ballot, first levy and collection years, and treatment of separate levy funds.
- Any board action establishing a 0.30% plan and the tax-base/collection assumptions behind the current pro forma.
- Current actual revenue/expenditure and cash-flow records if making a claim about a current operating deficit, service reductions, or a closure date.

Do not infer a present-day cash deficit by simply scaling the future ESD's tax revenue to 0.09%; district boundaries, taxable base, TIF treatment, contracts, billing and service plan can differ.

## Election-law wording

10 ILCS 5/9-25.1 bars using public funds to urge votes for or against a proposition while permitting factual proposition information. The local page therefore explains rates, source figures and limitations without a voting recommendation, unsupported motive accusation, or claim that the flyer is fraudulent. Whether particular organizational funds/communications fall under all applicable election rules is a matter for district counsel; this review is not legal certification.
