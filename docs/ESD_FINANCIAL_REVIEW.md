# Millstadt EMS financial records and service-continuity review

Reviewed October 2, 2026. Local review draft; nothing deployed.

**The records substantiate depleted money-market savings, overdue vendor bills, and a proposed budget that still has a funding gap. They do not establish a shutdown date, an inevitable closure following a No vote, or the absence of all legal responsibility for ambulance service.** A conditional statement about service reductions or closure if sufficient funding is not secured is supportable as the agency's stated risk, accompanied by the evidence and its limits.

## Scope and source checks

The live [Financial Transparency page](https://millstadtems.org/financials-information-hub) lists **90 documents**: 34 annual audits, 23 Form 990 filings, 22 county tax computation reports, four management-compensation reports, one bank-statement package, one budget pro forma, one settlement sheet, and four corporate/IRS/status records. The 68 locally hosted PDFs contain **1,477 pages**. The county reports were reviewed for their relevant Millstadt ambulance-fund pages, not unrelated districts.

The live page's document titles match the connected repository's registry. Downloads of the current pro forma, FY 2024–2025 audit, money-market package, and FY 2024–2025 management reports match the local originals byte for byte. All 22 county reports were downloaded from the county's published links. Scanned documents required OCR; figures used in the website's new financial discussion were checked against page images. The library's historical material was screened for financial context and relevant service/closure disclosures. This is a source and arithmetic review, not a new CPA audit or a legal certification. The companion inventory identifies each document and review coverage.

## What the records actually establish

### 1. A substantial reduction in one savings account

The [47-page money-market package](https://millstadtems.org/financial-transparency/bank-statements/money-market-account-2023-01-31-to-2026-08-31.pdf#page=1) shows:

| Date | Money-market balance |
| --- | ---: |
| January 31, 2023 | $201,826.25 |
| April 30, 2024 | $67,294.09 |
| April 30, 2025 | $97,504.46 |
| December 31, 2025 | $52,598.84 |
| April 30, 2026 | $34,327.20 |
| June 30, 2026 | $14,334.42 |
| August 31, 2026 | $14,338.08 |

January 2023 to August 2026: **$187,488.17 less, a 92.90% reduction**. The first two PDF pages show the comparison endpoints; the itemized packet provides the intervening activity. Some pages duplicate those endpoints and must not be counted as additional accounts or transactions.

The itemized statements identify payroll transfers, including $5,000 in May 2026 and $15,000 in June 2026. They also identify a $25,000 truck down payment in May 2025. Two temporary transfers were returned: $30,000 withdrawn in February 2024 was redeposited in October 2024, and $18,000 withdrawn in July 2025 was returned in August 2025. Those are not additional permanent losses. The balance increased by interest in July and August 2026 with no withdrawals shown for those months.

**Use:** savings have been drawn down, including to support payroll. **Do not say:** the service has only $14,338.08 in total cash, every dollar of the reduction was payroll, the account decreased every month, or the balance establishes an exhaustion date. Transfers between the service's accounts are not themselves consolidated operating expenses.

### 2. Past-due bills and debt obligations

The [pro forma, PDF page 7](https://millstadtems.org/financial-transparency/budgets/millstadt-ems-annual-budget-pro-forma.pdf#page=7), lists:

| Past-due item | Balance |
| --- | ---: |
| Accounting arrears | $28,000.00 |
| EMSMC catch-up | $28,330.66 |
| Mediclaims invoice | $10,000.00 |
| Total | **$66,330.66** |

The page's debt section rounds this total to $66,331 and states that payroll was prioritized, leaving some vendors unpaid. Its listed loan balances total $485,581 in rounded whole dollars, and the scheduled payments annualize to $106,112.11. Loan principal balances are not an annual operating deficit.

The pro forma excludes annual Zoll payments because it **assumes that loan is paid before service operations begin**. That explains the $17,779.03 difference between the page's annualized loan payments and the pro forma's $88,333.08. It also budgets **$0 for the $66,330.66 arrears** on the assumption they are paid beforehand. These assumptions need a funding source; they are not evidence of completed repayment.

A labeling issue remains in the PDF: its “Total disclosed debt” of $450,023.75 excludes the separately disclosed $35,558.06 Zoll balance. All the listed balances sum to $485,581.81. The $450,023.75 figure is the subtotal of obligations retained in the proposed annual budget, not the total of every disclosed loan. The source PDF was not altered during this review.

### 4. Collections and activity provide additional context

The August 13, 2026 collections snapshot reports **$441,847 collected against a $295,230 target**, a reported $146,617 positive variance, for January through partial August. The displayed monthly entries sum to $441,848 and a $146,618 variance—a $1 source-rounding discrepancy already distinguished by the page's calculated total. The snapshot is evidence of collections above that target, not proof that every expense is covered.

The billing tables include 911 and interfacility-transfer activity. FY 2025–2026 service-date billing reports $658,021.25 total revenue; the proposed 911-only budget projects $227,274.33 in 911 collections. Different service scope, posting dates, service dates and reporting cutoffs prevent a direct comparison. The partial May–July 2026 table must not be annualized as if it were a complete cash-collection period.

The Mediclaims FY 2024–2025 close reports charges, receipts, adjustments, total A/R and net A/R as different measures. Do not treat $1.717 million in charges or $440,106.33 total A/R as readily collectible bank cash. The vendor's $108,835 net A/R and the agency-wide audit's $396,096 net patient receivables are not reconciled by the posted records; different definitions and scope must be resolved before attributing a discrepancy to an error or loss.

### 5. The proposed budget remains short at 0.30%

The [September 30 pro forma](https://millstadtems.org/financial-transparency/budgets/millstadt-ems-annual-budget-pro-forma.pdf#page=1) already assumes **0.30% tax revenue** with 98% collection:

| Measure | Annual projection |
| --- | ---: |
| Property taxes | $1,031,072.11 |
| 911 billing | $227,274.33 |
| Total revenue | $1,258,346.44 |
| Service costs before replacement savings | $1,394,750.83 |
| Gap before replacement savings | **$136,404.39** |
| Annual replacement-savings contribution | $262,571.43 |
| Gap including replacement savings | **$398,975.82** |

These are **not** verified current losses at 0.09% or the calculated result of a No vote. The replacement contribution is savings for future purchases, not current-year operating spending. The $213,500 employee-benefits estimate is marked pending vendor estimates. The model also excludes arrears and Zoll payments as explained above. The tax-revenue projection implies a taxable base of approximately $350.705 million; the published Millstadt Fire District rate-setting EAV alone is $309.293 million. The proposed ESD includes additional territory, so the two tax bases must not be silently substituted for one another.

The pro forma includes **$59,844.68 IMRF**, Social Security, Medicare, workers' compensation and insurance as expenses. It lists no separate add-on tax revenue. The user's clarification is that the **0.30% total ESD plan includes those costs with no separate IMRF levy**. That plan should be stated plainly, while distinguishing it from future-board authority.

### 6. The county records show actual funding increases

All 22 published Millstadt ambulance-fund pages were checked. The 2025 certified ambulance rate is **0.0900%** and the listed local maximum is **0.2500%**. [2025 source, page 57](https://www.stclaircountyil.gov/webdocuments/departments/countyclerk/taxextensions/2025%20Tax%20Computation%20Reports.pdf#page=57).

The extension after TIF/EZ rose from **$239,809.55 in 2024 to $278,363.25 in 2025**, an increase of **$38,553.70, or 16.08%**. [2024 source, page 55](https://www.stclaircountyil.gov/webdocuments/departments/countyclerk/taxextensions/2024%20Tax%20Computation%20Reports.pdf#page=55). Levy requests were $207,500 for 2010–2019, $208,000 in 2020–2021, $218,400 in 2022, $228,293 in 2023, $239,734 in 2024 and $278,318 in 2025.

That does not establish that funding was adequate or that every requested increase was granted. It does mean that “no increases were provided” would be inaccurate. The posted library does not establish which specific larger requests were denied, their dates, amounts or the Fire District's reasons. “Meaningful” is a judgment, not a number the tax reports prove.

The separate 2025 settlement sheet's $238,525.85 covers three distributions combining corporate and ambulance funds. It is not the annual ambulance extension and cannot replace the $278,363.25 figure.

### 7. Expense, compensation and organizational records

The FY 2025–2026 posted monthly entries total $612,797.79 for ADP payroll-related outflows, $153,950.43 for operations, $75,467.28 for building/communications, $68,472.46 for professional fees, and $52,754.33 for truck repairs. Together these selected tables total **$963,442.29**. This is not a verified complete income statement: principal repayments, credit-card payments, payroll components and possible overlapping categories need ledger reconciliation. The separately reported $3,995.52 uniform purchase has an unspecified “prior-year” period and was not added again.

The page correctly distinguishes monthly-entry arithmetic from worksheet YTD amounts: Stryker 2 differs by $0.08; WEX by $0.50; and Ameren by $260.29. The building subtotal is consequently $260.29 higher than the supplied worksheet subtotal. May–August 2026 partial truck-repair entries total $14,724.67; warranty work should not be assigned a made-up additional cost.

The four management reports distinguish payroll compensation, recorded work or scheduled-entry hours, leave and hypothetical hourly equivalents. Their totals must not be added to the service-wide payroll tables as a separate expense. Posted nursing/EMT/paramedic rates are not a substitute for the individual management compensation records. Neither historical compensation nor headcount by itself establishes future required staffing or cash runway.

IRS reinstatement is effective September 15, 2025, retroactive to the revocation date stated in the April 20, 2026 letter. The Secretary of State certificate is dated August 31, 2026; the Attorney General letter dated September 24, 2026 says reporting is current through April 30, 2025. These establish the statuses stated in the documents, **not solvency, guaranteed funding or a guarantee of future service**.

## Legal and service-continuity conclusions

- **No vote and closure:** No would leave this proposed ESD unformed. Insufficient funding could lead the existing agency to reduce operations or close, as the agency reports. A ballot result does not itself order closure. The financial library does not contain a complete current cash-flow forecast, adopted shutdown plan, replacement contract or response-time study establishing a date or guaranteed outcome.
- **Existing statutory responsibility:** [70 ILCS 705/22(a)(3)](https://ilga.gov/Documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/007007050K22.htm) imposes a responsibility on fire protection districts to cause ambulance service to be provided where adequate and continuing service does not exist. It is inaccurate to turn the “not essential” designation argument into “no legal service responsibility.” That duty does not establish that Millstadt EMS itself, a particular station location or unchanged arrival times are guaranteed.
- **Coverage rules:** [77 Ill. Admin. Code 515.810(e), (f), (h)](https://www.ilga.gov/commission/jcar/admincode/077/077005150F08100R.html) addresses response commitments, 24-hour coverage and written mutual-aid/backup arrangements. Outside response could take longer depending on location and availability; the posted records do not quantify a delay or establish a specific replacement arrangement.
- **Essential-service legislation:** [HB5133](https://ilga.gov/Legislation/BillStatus?DocNum=5133&DocTypeID=HB&GAID=18&LegId=166724&Print=1&SessionID=114) proposes an essential-service designation and broader obligations. The reviewed status lists referral to Rules, not enactment. The 2012 task-force report and HR872 are historical advocacy/context, not substitutes for current law or evidence that today's existing duties disappear.
- **IMRF:** [40 ILCS 5/7-171](https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/fulltext?DocName=004000050K7-171) authorizes a separate tax in applicable circumstances; it does not impose an automatic extra 0.10%. [IMRF guidance](https://www.imrf.org/AAmanual/online_AA_Manual/4.15.htm) describes transfers from operating funds. State the agency's no-add-on plan; do not claim every future separate levy would be illegal.
- **Other fire-district taxing authority:** Current [section 22](https://ilga.gov/Documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/007007050K22.htm) contains differing provisions, including 0.40% in (b-5)/(c) and 0.30% in other provisions. Describing a universal statewide 0.30% ceiling is too broad. The county's published local 0.2500% maximum remains the directly relevant evidence for the flyer's local-capacity assertion.
- **Public election information:** [10 ILCS 5/9-25.1](https://www.ilga.gov/Documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/001000050K9-25.1.htm) distinguishes factual proposition information from using public funds to urge a vote. The site draft presents sourced facts without voting advice. Final institutional legal compliance depends on the entity, funding and communications, and is not certified by this review.

## What was changed locally

The Election Information page now embeds the same calculator, referendum explanations, financial evidence, flyer photo and parcel comparison as the calculator page, with jump links and the existing county voting resources. The fixed comparison is 0.09% to 0.30%; 0.30% − 0.09% = **0.21 percentage points**, or $21 per $10,000 taxable value annually. For the reviewed parcel, $317.70 after minus $95.31 before gives **$222.39 more per year** under unchanged-value/replacement assumptions. The $424 flyer figure matches the full 0.40% levy rounded; it is not the planned net increase.

The new text preserves both the financial warning signs and the positive historical audit/collection results. It does not invent a closure date, replacement guarantee, denied-request record or future-board commitment. No source financial PDF was modified, and nothing was pushed to GitHub or deployed to Vercel.
