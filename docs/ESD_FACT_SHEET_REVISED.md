# Millstadt EMS Emergency Services District
## Referendum facts and tax comparison

**November 3, 2026 general election - review draft, October 2, 2026**

### What are voters being asked to decide?

The supplied ballot asks whether to create the Millstadt EMS Emergency Services District and authorize a base property-tax levy of up to **0.40%**. The ballot authorizes a rate; it does not require the district to use the maximum.

### What rate does the EMS financial plan use?

Millstadt EMS states that its plan uses a **0.30% total ESD property-tax rate, with no separate IMRF levy added on top**. The EMS Average Annual 911 Budget Pro Forma uses that rate and already includes IMRF and other employer costs. With its assumed 98% collection factor, the plan projects **$1,031,072.11 per year in property-tax revenue**. This is a planning assumption, not a binding cap or a guaranteed future rate. The district's future board and the county tax-extension process determine the actual levy and billed rate.

### What value is taxed?

- **Full home value:** what the home and land together might sell for. This is not the mortgage balance.
- **EAV, or equalized assessed value:** the smaller value used to start the tax calculation. For an ordinary home, assessment starts at one-third of full value, then equalization may adjust it.
- **Taxable value:** EAV after approved exemptions. This is the value to multiply by the tax rate. If using the final taxable value on a tax bill, do not divide by three or subtract exemptions again.

For example, a $300,000 home with an equalization factor of 1 has an estimated EAV of $100,000. Subtracting an example $6,000 exemption leaves $94,000 of taxable value. At 0.09%, the annual EMS tax is $84.60. At 0.30%, it is $282.00, a $197.40 annual increase if the old levy is replaced. Use the actual approved exemptions for your property; not every property qualifies for the same amount.

### What happens to the existing 0.09% ambulance levy?

The comparison uses **0.09% before** and **0.30% after**, on the same taxable value. It assumes the existing ambulance levy is replaced by the ESD levy. The rate difference is **0.21 percentage points**, or **$21 annually for each $10,000 of taxable value**.

Section 2 of the Emergency Services Districts Act conditions overlapping ambulance service on a Fire District resolution to cease its ambulance levy under section 22 of the Fire Protection District Act and on the ESD operating ambulance service in that territory. The election alone does not establish the effective tax-bill year. The Fire District's separate fire-protection taxes would not be removed by this comparison.

### What area is described in the supplied ballot?

The Millstadt Fire Protection District; the Hecker Fire Protection District portion in Millstadt Township, excluding its Prairie du Long Township portion; and the Waterloo Community Fire Protection District portion in St. Clair County. The Smithton Fire Protection District is not named in this description. Confirm a parcel against the certified boundaries; mailing addresses alone do not establish membership.

### What would the proposed budget support?

The plan includes EMS personnel and coverage, ambulances, medical supplies and medications, equipment, training, facilities, debt payments, and savings for future vehicles and equipment. These are proposed budget uses, not guarantees of a particular response time or purchase date.

### What do the current financial records show?

The posted bank statements show the money-market account decreasing from **$201,826.25 on January 31, 2023 to $14,338.08 on August 31, 2026**—a **$187,488.17 reduction, about 93%**. The account was used for payroll transfers and a truck down payment. This is one savings account, not the service's total cash or a cash-runway forecast.

The September 30 pro forma lists **$66,330.66 in overdue vendor bills**. It assumes these bills and the Zoll monitor loan will be paid before proposed service operations begin and excludes those payments from annual spending. They should not be described as already paid.

The latest completed audit covers the year ending April 30, 2025 and reports **$974,719 revenue, $876,668 expenses and a $98,051 increase in net assets**. It reports $141,516 in cash and cash equivalents at that earlier date. Net assets include more than cash. The FY 2025–2026 audit remains listed as awaiting completion. The August 13, 2026 collections report also shows collections above its target. These results should be presented alongside the evidence of depleted savings and arrears.

Taken together, the records support concern about financial resilience. They do not establish a closure date, probability of closure or a complete current cash position. See the separate financial-records review for source pages and reconciliations.

### What do 'Yes' and 'No' mean?

A **Yes** result would create the ESD under the Act and authorize the base levy described in the ballot. It would not legally fix the planning rate at 0.30% or establish that the budget is fully funded.

A **No** result would mean the proposed ESD is not created through this referendum. It would not itself close ambulance service. It also would not guarantee a particular provider, contract, staffing level or future funding arrangement. Those depend on separate decisions and available resources. Millstadt EMS reports that inadequate funding could require reductions or closure if sufficient funding is not secured through this proposal or another arrangement. That is a conditional risk, not an inevitable result or a documented shutdown date.

### Is replacement ambulance service guaranteed?

The reviewed records do not establish an adopted replacement plan, guaranteed station location or unchanged arrival times. However, section 22(a)(3) of the Fire Protection District Act imposes a duty to cause ambulance service to be provided where adequate and continuing service does not exist. Illinois EMS rules also address coverage, response commitments and mutual aid. The “essential service” label does not erase those duties or prove that there is no service responsibility.

An ambulance responding from farther away or already on another call could take longer. The effect cannot be quantified without staffing, location, dispatch and call data. No specific additional delay is established by the reviewed materials.

### Is 0.40% a ceiling on every possible district tax?

No. It is the base-levy limit in this organizational ballot question. Illinois law provides separate authority for certain eligible retirement, Social Security/Medicare and liability costs, and the ESD Act contains separate additional referendum provisions. Those authorities do not create an automatic 0.10% surcharge or prove that a future combined rate will be 0.50%. The calculator models the rates explicitly shown and does not silently add unadopted levies.

### A checked example from the photographed flyer

For **305 Traver Tine Circle**, parcel **12-10.0-414-002**, the county's **2025 tax record, payable 2026**, shows $118,899 EAV, $13,000 total exemptions and **$105,899 taxable value**.

| Scenario | Full annual EMS levy | Increase over 0.09% |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Current 0.09% | $95.31 | — |
| Planned 0.30% | $317.70 | $222.39 |
| Ballot base-levy limit 0.40% | $423.60 | $328.29 |

The flyer's handwritten **$424** is consistent with the **full 0.40% levy rounded to whole dollars**, after exemptions. It is not the net increase at the 0.30% planning rate. Under the comparison assumptions, that increase is **$222.39 annually**, or **$18.53 per month equivalent**. This is an illustration using one tax year's value, not a guarantee of the future bill.
